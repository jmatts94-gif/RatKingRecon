Privacy Policy

Short version: your game lives on your phone. Nothing to sign up for. Play stats are anonymous, and only if you say yes. If you use Google Play Games, your Play Games name and best World Boss hit each week can appear on the game's leaderboard, and a copy of your save is kept in your own Google account.

Last updated 12 November 2026 (cloud save update)

🐀 Who this covers

This policy covers Burrow Kings, a game by RTK Studios, an independent developer in the UK (contact below). It covers the Android app and the web version.

The person responsible for your data (the "data controller") is Jacob Matts, trading as RTK Studios.

💾 Your game and your save

  • Your progress, settings and knight name are saved on your device.
  • Your knight's name is never sent to us and never shown to other players. It only travels inside your own save.
  • Android backup: if backup is turned on in your phone's settings, Android may copy the game's save to your Google account, so a new phone or a reinstall gets your progress back. This is Google's own backup. We can't see it. You can turn it off in your phone's settings.
  • Cloud save (Google Play Games): if you are signed in to Google Play Games, the game also keeps a copy of your save in your own Google account (Play Games "Saved Games"), so you can carry on from another phone. The copy holds your game progress, your knight's name and your game settings. Nothing else about you. We can't see it or open it. The game always asks before it swaps the save on your phone for the cloud one. To stop it, sign out of Play Games. You can delete the cloud copy in your Google Play Games settings.
  • Uninstalling the game deletes the save on your device.

📊 Anonymous play stats (only if you say yes)

The first time you play, the game asks: "Share anonymous play stats?" The game plays the same whichever you choose. You can change your answer any time in Settings > Share play stats.

If you say yes, the game sends things like:

  • When the game opens, and how far you've got (for example, runs played and the chapter you've reached).
  • Fights won or lost, and the game mode.
  • When you open chests or the shop, watch an ad, or buy something (which item, never payment details).
  • When you open the Great Cat's Tally, and the damage number when a score is sent to it.
  • Your device type (for example, Android), the game version, and whether it's a test build.

Each set of stats carries a random ID made on your device. It isn't linked to your name, email, account or advertising ID.

We don't collect your name, email, knight name, location or contacts. The stats service is told not to store your IP address or look up where you are.

Who handles it: PostHog, on servers in the EU. See PostHog's privacy policy.

Why: to find where the game is too hard, confusing or dull, and fix it. The legal basis is your consent.

How long: at most 2 years, then deleted.

Turning it off: when you switch it off, the game stops sending stats and deletes its random ID and any unsent stats from your device. Stats already sent can't be traced back to you, so we can't pick out yours to delete them.

🏆 Google Play Games and the Great Cat's Tally

The Great Cat's Tally is a weekly leaderboard of the best hits on the World Boss. It uses Google Play Games. The rest of the game works fully without it.

  • Signing in: if you already use Google Play Games on your phone, it may sign you in by itself when the game starts. If not, you're only asked when you tap Sign in on the Tally.
  • What's sent: after each World Boss fight, the game sends your damage number to Google Play Games. Google keeps your best for the week.
  • What other players see: your Play Games name (and Play Games picture, if Google shows one), your rank and your score. Never your real name, email or knight name.
  • What we get: we can see the Tally and its names and scores, like any player. We don't get your email or your Google account details. The game doesn't store your Play Games ID.

Who handles it: Google. See Google's privacy policy.

Why: to run the leaderboard you choose to join.

How long: each Tally resets every week. Google keeps your Play Games data, including scores, until you delete it.

Turning it off: don't sign in, or sign out of Play Games. You can change who sees your profile, or delete your Play Games data, in your Google Play Games settings.

📺 Optional ads

You can choose to watch a short ad for a small reward, up to 3 a day. Ads never play unless you tap to watch one.

Ads come from Google AdMob. In the UK and EU, the game asks for your consent first. When an ad plays, AdMob may collect:

  • Your device's advertising ID (you can reset or delete it in your phone's settings).
  • Basic device info, such as the model, Android version and IP address.
  • How you interact with the ad.

Google uses this to show ads and measure them. See how Google uses data from apps that use its services.

🛒 Purchases and chests

The game sells optional items, like Gnawstones and a one-time Starter Pack. Google Play handles all payments. We never see or store your card or payment details. We only get a confirmation from Google Play that the purchase went through.

Some items, like Royal chests, give random rewards. You can see the odds for every chest in the game, on the Chests tab.

🚫 What this game does not do

  • No account of our own, and no sign-up. Google Play Games sign-in is optional.
  • No forced ads.
  • No selling or renting of your data.
  • No location tracking.
  • The only outside services are PostHog (anonymous stats, if you say yes) and Google (backup, ads, purchases and Play Games).

🧒 Children

Burrow Kings isn't made for children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from children.

⚖️ Your rights

Under UK and EU data law you can ask to see, correct or delete your personal data, and you can withdraw consent at any time (for stats, use the Settings switch). Most of what the game keeps is on your own device, where you control it. Email us with any request.

If you're unhappy with how we handle your data, you can complain to the UK's Information Commissioner's Office: ico.org.uk.
